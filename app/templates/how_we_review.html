How we review, and what we won't do

A check is one person reading your whole document and telling you, plainly, what to fix. Here's what that covers, and where we draw the line.

Who reads your document

A reviewer on our team reads it start to finish. Your notes are written by that person, not generated by software. If something's unclear, they ask you on your document's page before guessing, and the clock pauses while they wait for your answer.

What every review covers

  • The brief. Whether it answers the question, and meets the word limit and style you give us.
  • Structure and argument. A clear main point, paragraphs that each do one job, and an order that builds.
  • Evidence and citations. Claims that need a source, and citations that match your reference list.
  • Clarity and correctness. Grammar, spelling, punctuation and tone, marked line by line.

You get short notes that put the most important fix first, and your own file marked up. Ask your reviewer about any note on your document's page; replies are free.

What we won't do

  • Write, rewrite or finish your work, or add content to it.
  • Complete assignments, quizzes or exams for anyone.
  • Change your argument. We'll tell you where it's weak; how you fix it is up to you.
  • Produce plagiarism, similarity or AI-detection scores. A check is a human read, not a software scan.
  • Promise a grade. Your marker decides that.

That line matters. Feedback that leaves the writing to you is what most universities allow. Some ask you to declare any proofreading, so check your institution's policy.

Your files

Your document is stored privately so your reviewer can read it. Only you and our review team can open it. It's never added to any database, never shared, and never used to train AI.

Your work, checked. Still yours.
