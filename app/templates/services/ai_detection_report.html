How to read the numbers

An AI score is a probability, not proof. Detectors do flag human writing, and your marker should look at more than a number. A similarity score isn't a plagiarism verdict either: a properly quoted and cited passage still counts as a match.

What matters is what's behind the score. If a passage is flagged, check that every borrowed idea is cited and every quote is marked. If your own writing is flagged, your drafts and notes are your best evidence. More in what to do if you're flagged and similarity scores, explained.

Is my document stored?

It's kept privately in your account so you can open your report, and only you and our team can see it. It's never added to a plagiarism database, never shared and never used to train AI.

My own writing was flagged. What now?

Keep your drafts, notes and version history: they show how the work was written. Ask your tutor how the score is being used. Our flagged work review goes through each flagged passage with you, and our guide covers what to do next.

Will you rewrite my work so it scores lower?

No. We show you what a detector sees and which matches need citing; the writing stays yours.
